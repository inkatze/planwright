- 2026-09-02 [planwright] Operator decision 2026-09-02: the sign-off act should be the MERGE, not the draft->ready flip. Today gate-wiring defines approval as "the draft->ready flip with the commit still present is the approval", which is the source of the legibility problem recorded in pending-sign-off-legibility: the flip is an invisible approval that a human can perform for unrelated reasons (CI went green, ready to share) without realizing they just approved every marked commit. Moving the semantics to merge fixes this at the root rather than by adding warnings: merge is already the one irreversible, unambiguously human act in the framework, people already read merging as accepting the change, and it needs no new affordance to be legible. Consequences to work through if adopted: the pending-sign-off checklist becomes a pre-MERGE checklist rather than a pre-ready one; ready-flip authorization (planwright#379) decouples cleanly from approval authorization, so an agent flipping ready no longer approves anything and the #379 default can simply be agent-may-flip without the no-pending-sign-off carve-out; and a reviewer rejecting an item still reverts its commit, only now before merging rather than before the flip. Related: planwright#379, and the pending-sign-off-legibility observation this supersedes the remedy section of.
