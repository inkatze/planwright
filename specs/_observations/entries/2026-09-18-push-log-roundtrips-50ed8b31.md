- 2026-09-18 [planwright] The away push pays one event-log re-exec per item, outside the fleet lock, and that is now the whole cost of an away pass with a large backlog. Measured on a fixture where every item blocks and every item is due a first push: on the `none` channel, 10 items = 1.6s wall for the away `next`, 200 items = 50.8s; on `push`, 2.9s and 65.6s. Skipping the transport fork on the channels that have no transport (`none`, `statusline`) removed one fork per item and moved the number very little, which is the measurement that identifies the dominant term: `log_event` re-execs tower-queue.sh, which resolves four knobs and retakes the fleet lock for its own append, so N lines is N round-trips of ~15 processes each. Same per-line publish path the open threads on PR #471 raise for the settling pass; one batched publish would cover both callers. The settling pass's lock hold is unmoved by any of it (0.048s at 10 items, 0.055s at 200, against the 2s stated bound), because none of this work is inside the critical section, and `counts` — which the status line calls at Claude Code's own cadence — is 0.028s over a 200-item store. Bounded in practice: an item pushes at most twice per away episode, so a steady tower loop pushes zero or one per pass and only a cold start against a large backlog pays the tail.
