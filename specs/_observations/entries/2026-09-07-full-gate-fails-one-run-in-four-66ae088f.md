- 2026-09-07 [planwright] The full `mise run check` gate fails roughly ONE RUN IN FOUR on this machine, on suites unrelated to whatever is being changed, and the failures do not reproduce in isolation. MEASURED across 43 full-gate runs in one session: 13 red. Three of those were genuine defects in the change under test and were fixed (test-allocation-launch-wiring.sh once, test-check-instructions.sh twice). The remaining TEN were not: test-fleet-streamjson.sh x4 (all "c12: queue should be clear after the answer"), test-fleet-registration.sh x4 (mostly "e8: a signalled register left the shared fleet lock held"), test-fleet-state.sh x2. Every one passed in isolation immediately afterwards, and a re-run of the gate passed. That is a ~23% false-red rate on a gate that is the merge bar for every pull request, which makes CI on any PR close to a coin flip and is a large part of why merging moved slowly this session. THE FAILURES ARE NOT ONE BUG: three different suites, and within test-fleet-registration.sh the e8 case is about the shared advisory lock surviving a signal while the streamjson c12 case is about an attention queue row -- different subsystems, same trigger. WHAT DOES NOT REPRODUCE THEM: running the suite alone (3/3 clean), running six copies of the same suite concurrently (0/6 failures on both main's lock code and the symlink-lock branch's), and widening the specific windows a proposed mechanism named (see the c12 entry: 0 failures with a 3-second window). Only the real gate does it, which runs 12 DIFFERENT suites in parallel, several of which spawn background supervisors and take filesystem locks under the same fleet home shape. That difference -- concurrent DIFFERENT workloads rather than N copies of one -- is the untested hypothesis worth starting from. FOR WHOEVER TAKES IT: do not chase the individual cases, three sessions have now done that and produced observations calling each one a flake. Instrument the gate itself: capture, at the moment a suite fails inside a real parallel run, what else was running, what was in the shared fleet home, and the loaded-machine state. The per-case observations already exist and none of them explains a 23% rate across three unrelated suites.
