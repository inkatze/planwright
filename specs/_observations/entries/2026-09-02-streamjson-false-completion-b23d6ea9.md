- 2026-09-02 [planwright] fleet-streamjson.sh status reporting "completed result=success" does not mean the dispatched unit finished its work. Observed 2026-09-02: worker flc-task-3 (fleet-lifecycle-closure Task 3) reported completed/success while its branch carried 2 unpushed commits, no PR, and its final assistant message was a mid-flight self-review findings report — /execute-task never reached its terminal push-and-draft-PR step. CORRECTION after further observation: the worker was never finished at all. Its process (pid 916618) remained ALIVE and continued issuing permission requests for many minutes AFTER status began reporting completed/success, so this is not a truncated-run artifact. The supervisor latches the first result event it sees as the run outcome and never revises it while the worker keeps working; the result record it latched carried num_turns=2 and duration_ms=30939, a 31-second two-turn segment, while the worker event stream spanned about ninety minutes and kept growing. The defect is therefore stronger than an early report: status asserts completion and stays wrong while the worker is demonstrably still running, so a tower that trusts it frees the unit's slot and may dispatch over a live worker. Why this is worse than a crash: the reconcile sweep can only orphan an in-flight unit on positive evidence of death, and this worker exited cleanly, so the sweep will not rescue it either — the unit derives complete, the tower frees the slot, and the work sits stranded on an unpushed branch. Attended, it was caught only because the tower checked for a PR that never appeared. Candidate fix: completion must be derived from the unit contract rather than the process outcome — a worker is done when its branch is pushed and its draft PR exists (the evidence orchestrate-state.sh already reads), not when its supervisor sees a success result. At minimum, status should report the process outcome and the unit outcome as separate fields so a caller cannot mistake one for the other. Related irony: the finding this worker was writing when it was cut off is that the registry self-heal its best-effort contract promises ("the next scan reconciles it") does not exist — no scan ever appends a registry record.
