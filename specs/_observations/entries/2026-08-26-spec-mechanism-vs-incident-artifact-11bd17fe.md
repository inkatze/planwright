- 2026-08-26 [repo] A requirement can specify a detection mechanism that provably cannot detect the incident it was written for, when the mechanism is derived from a prose gloss of the incident rather than from the incident's actual artifact. release-hardening REQ-H1.2 mandates failing a release proposal whose CHANGELOG.md diff adds entries "at or below the latest release tag", compared by SemVer precedence via rl_latest_release_tag / rl_version_gt. But PR #339, the incident it cites, adds exactly ONE version heading -- ## [0.34.0], strictly ABOVE the then-latest v0.33.0 -- and carries its 124 already-released commits as bullets underneath. The mandated comparison passes #339. The ambiguity rides on "entry": obs:fd6c2f4f and the task Done-when use it to mean a changelog bullet, while REQ-H1.2 and test-spec's third fixture use it to mean a version-headed section. Both readings are internally coherent and they are different checks. Worth a kickoff lens-checklist item: when a REQ specifies a detection mechanism against a cited incident, verify the mechanism against the incident's real artifact (gh pr diff, the actual log) during the requirements walkthrough, not against its narrative summary. Cheap to check, and it is exactly the class of defect that survives to execution because every prose reading of the bundle is self-consistent.
