- 2026-09-14 [planwright] An attended tower session interrupted its operator about ten times in one run, and most of those interruptions were not required by any rule. docs/fleet.md already states that Needs-sign-off findings MAY be decided unattended: the fix lands on the branch in its own commit and the human approves at PR review by leaving or reverting it. That is the same pending-sign-off convention the repo already runs, visible in the commits on two PRs open at the time. The tower nonetheless presented every Needs-sign-off item as a question first - thirty findings on one PR, four contract changes on another - turning an apply-then-review gate into an ask-then-apply one. Roughly half the runs interruptions were that misreading; two or three more were genuine forks that --unattended would have recorded to tasks.md Awaiting input for a single read rather than a conversation each; only two were escalations the policy actually reserves for a human, both in the hard-disqualifier zone around locking and security. Two separate defects hide in this. The first is reading Needs-sign-off as needs-asking, when the bucket name describes where approval happens, not whether work stops. The second is that an attended run has no mechanism pushing back toward autonomy: --unattended changes what the skill does at its decision points but nothing changes what the session does at a turn boundary, so a tower that has just verified a result writes a summary and stops even when it can name its own next three steps. Worth considering whether the attended path should state the apply-then-review default as loudly as the unattended one does, since attended is the mode a human is watching and therefore the one where asking feels free and is not.
