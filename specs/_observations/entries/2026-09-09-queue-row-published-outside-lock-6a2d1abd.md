- 2026-09-09 [planwright] An answered request could stay in the operator decision queue, because the queue row is a projection of journal state that both writers derived and published OUTSIDE the lock protecting that state. In fleet-streamjson.sh, handle_line appended the receipt under journal_lock, unlocked, then published the queue item; cmd_answer marked the row answered under the lock, unlocked, then cleared the queue. Interleave them and handle_line reads pending, answer flips to answered and clears the row, and handle_line's write lands on top -- a lost update that re-posts a decision the operator already made. Operator-visible consequence: the queue shows a decision that has been made, and answering it again hits the already-answered/undeliverable refusal, which is exactly the "no request may pend permanently unanswerable" property c12 exists to protect. HOW IT WAS FOUND, which is the transferable part: test-fleet-streamjson.sh c12 "queue should be clear after the answer" had been failing the full parallel gate while passing in isolation, and was written off as a load flake more than once. The assertion asserted a value without reporting it. Making it print what it actually saw diagnosed the bug in two runs: it killed the first hypothesis immediately (the count was 1, not the empty string a suppressed queue subcommand would give) and then handed over the mechanism, because the dump showed the journal reading answered at T and the attention store still reading awaiting-input at T+1 -- a store write that postdated the answer it contradicted. A bare boolean assertion on a racy invariant costs more in repeated archaeology than the dump costs to write. FIRST FIX WAS WRONG and the wrongness is instructive: re-deriving from the journal instead of publishing the captured id (attention_settled rather than attention_upsert) narrowed the window without closing it -- the store timestamp moved from one second after the answer to equal to it -- because the read and the write were still not atomic with respect to the other writer. Deriving is necessary (the row is meant to carry the OLDEST still-pending request, not the newest) but not sufficient; the derive-and-publish has to happen under the lock. Two things make holding it safe: journal_lock is a non-reentrant mkdir lock and nothing reachable from attention_settled re-takes it (journal_oldest_pending is an unlocked read), and fleet-attention.sh contains no reference back into fleet-streamjson.sh, so the journal-then-attention ordering cannot invert. STILL OPEN: cmd_alarm_scan reads the journal unlocked and upserts unlocked, so it carries the same race shape. It was left alone deliberately -- its job is re-escalating still-pending items out of band, so the right posture there needs its own thought rather than a reflexive lock. OUTCOME: moving derive-and-publish inside the journal lock at all three sites took the streamjson suite from 4/4 failures (including on main) to 4/4 passes across clean sequential gate runs. Passing runs narrow a race rather than disprove one, so this is evidence and not proof. Two things safe about holding the lock across the shell-out: journal_lock is a non-reentrant mkdir lock and nothing reachable from attention_settled re-takes it, and fleet-attention.sh has zero references back into fleet-streamjson.sh, so journal-then-attention cannot invert.
