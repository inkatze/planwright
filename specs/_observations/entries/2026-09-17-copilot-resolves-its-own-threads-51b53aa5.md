- 2026-09-17 [planwright] GitHub Copilot can resolve its own review thread, so an unresolved-thread count of zero is not proof that a review was drained. On PR #471 the thread about owe_log doing O(N) lock round-trips was deliberately left unresolved by the worker, with a reply explaining that the fix needed a change to the log verb and belonged in its own unit. It later showed as resolved, and the GraphQL resolvedBy field names Copilot, not the worker (every other resolution on that PR reads resolvedBy=inkatze). The worker noticed the discrepancy itself and flagged it in its report rather than accepting the lower count. Two consequences. First, a nested copilot-review loop that treats "unresolved == 0" as convergence can terminate while a real finding is outstanding and nobody has acted on it; convergence should be judged on what the run itself resolved, tracked by thread id, not on the remote count. Second, a deliberately parked finding can silently stop being visible on the PR, which is the whole point of parking it; here the substance survived only because a later round raised the same mechanism from the ordering angle in a separate thread. Worth having /copilot-review record the ids it resolves and compare them against the resolved set at convergence, reporting any it did not resolve itself.
