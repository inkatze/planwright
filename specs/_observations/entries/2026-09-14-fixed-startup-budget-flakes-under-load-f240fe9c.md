- 2026-09-14 [planwright] tests/test-fleet-streamjson.sh case 20 waits a fixed ten seconds for a worker to fork its hold loop, and that budget loses to a busy host rather than to any defect. Observed while verifying an unrelated lock-library branch: the case failed on a machine where another worktree was running its own full suite concurrently, and in the same run lint:shell took 746 seconds against a normal ten. The branch under test touched two files, neither referenced by fleet-streamjson, and nothing in the tree sources the library at all, so there was no path from the change to the failure - it was the fixed wait losing a race with the machine. The cost is not the flake itself but what it does to judgement: a red gate that is nobody fault trains a reader to explain failures away, which is the opposite of what a gate is for, and it lands hardest exactly when the fleet is busy and several worktrees run suites at once. This is the fixed-sleep-and-no-injectable-clock shape the bundle already names as a test-flakiness class. Worth either an injectable clock, a budget that scales with observed load, or a wait on the condition rather than on a duration.
