- 2026-09-12 [planwright] fleet_max_parallel_units counts WORKERS, not the load each one generates, and a /execute-task worker is not a unit of load: it runs the full 176-file suite through an xargs fan-out, twice or more (once per convergence pass, again on the final tree). Four concurrent workers plus a tower took this host to load average 58 with 247 claude-related processes (memory was never the constraint: 24 GB still available). The consequence is not slowness, it is WRONG VERDICTS that park finished work: on 2026-09-12 the tower-comms task-7 worker had its final check fail on tests/test-fleet-streamjson.sh c20, a ten-second wait for a shim child, and parked the unit Awaiting input; the format-grammar task-7 worker had its whole xargs pool killed by a signal with about forty files never reporting, which classify-ci-failure.sh default-classified as logic; the operator-dialogue task-10 worker had its runner scratch directory vanish mid-run. Every one of those units is sound, and GitHub's reference runner passed the sibling branches green. So the admission bound needs a load dimension, not just a count: either derive the bound from available cores against the suite's own parallelism, or serialise the convergence check across in-flight units (one suite at a time, fleet-wide) so a worker's verdict is never a function of how many siblings it happens to share a host with.
