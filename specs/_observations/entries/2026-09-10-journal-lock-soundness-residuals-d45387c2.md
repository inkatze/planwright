- 2026-09-10 [planwright] Three residual soundness gaps in fleet-streamjson.sh journal locking, found in review after the queue-projection race fix (#438) started holding the lock across a shell-out. None is caused by that fix; it made two of them reachable and the third visible. Recorded as a follow-up because each is a change to primitives shared with the launch and recover flows, not to the projection fix, and the projection fix is measured good on its own (c12 passing 7 of 7 against 4 of 4 failures before, main included). ONE: lock_take publishes the holder pid AFTER its mkdir and tolerates the write failing (printf ... || :), so acquisition can report success with the lock unstamped. A holder paused in that window, or a filesystem that fills between the two operations, leaves a lock whose holder cannot be read; a waiter then takes the age fallback and enters while the original is still inside the critical section. Acquisition should fail closed until an owner token is published, or the pre-stamp window should be fenced. TWO: because lock_take refuses to break a live holder by design, a wedged fleet-attention.sh cannot be reclaimed at journal_lock_stale at all. Other journal writers spin their 50x100ms budget, report busy, and stay out for as long as the shell-out hangs, so an attention-store hang becomes a journal outage for that worker. Bounding it needs a kill-safe execution or a separate recovery design. Worth noting the comment in that file claimed the stale break bounded this stall; that claim was true of the age-only lock it replaced and false afterwards, and has been corrected. THREE: cmd_stop reaches release_locks, which iterates lock_classes (journal.lock among them) and rm -rf s each one unconditionally, with no ownership check. An independent `answer` can be inside the shell-out when stop runs, so stop deletes a live holder lock, runs journal_close and the clear, and lets the answer publish its already-derived row afterwards -- recreating exactly the stale queue row the projection fix exists to prevent. So the critical section is not an exclusion boundary against stop, and any claim that the lock closes the projection race has to carry that exception until stop coordinates with the ownership-aware primitive.
