- 2026-09-03 [planwright] fleet-lifecycle-closure Task 4: the stop verb's self-close refusal decides 'am I inside the tree I am closing' from two seeds that fail in opposite directions, and two residual holes survive the version that shipped. First, the wide/narrow verdict tests whether ps accepted -ww, not whether argv was actually truncated; a ps that takes the flag and still caps the args column would be read as trustworthy, and the refusal would be skipped on a host that cannot support it. The current shape is safe only because the _supervise <worker> <dir> triple sits at the FRONT of the supervisor's argv, ahead of the init-message path and the CLI arguments, so a length cap cannot remove it - that ordering is load-bearing for a security property and nothing records it at the spawn site. Second, and more reachable: worker.pid never carries the _supervise argv (stop_candidates says so itself), so on a wide-argv host the inference 'no supervisor row means the recorded pids are recycled' is sound for supervisor.pid and false for worker.pid. Supervisor-dead-worker-alive is a first-class modeled state - cmd_recover exists for exactly it - and a close invoked from inside such an orphaned worker is not refused: the worker and the closer's ancestors are excluded from the kill set, the closer's siblings under the worker are not, and the close signals them and then reports stopped. Both holes close the same way the pid-reuse exposure does, with a start-time stamp binding a recorded pid to the worker that recorded it, which this bundle already defers to the supervisor-hardening work. Worth pricing that deferral against three separate defects now rather than treating them as independent.
