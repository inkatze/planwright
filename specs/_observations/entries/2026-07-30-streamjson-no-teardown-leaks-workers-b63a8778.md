- 2026-07-30 [planwright] fleet-streamjson.sh has no teardown subcommand and never has: as of 0.32.1 the surface is launch, answer, recover, alarm-scan, status (plus the internal _supervise). There is no stop, kill, reap, or shutdown, so a worker lifecycle has a documented beginning and no documented end, and the children ignore SIGTERM sent to the supervisor -- a parent kill does not reliably take them down. The practical consequence reported by the operator: finished stream-json-persistent workers accumulate leaked `claude -p` processes until someone pkills them by pattern, and GDS towers reached roughly 95 cumulative hours of leaked worker time before anyone noticed, because nothing in the fleet oracle counts them. A refinement from the 0.32.1 tower on 2026-07-29/30 that sharpens the diagnosis rather than contradicting it: three workers (guard-coverage task-1, merge-currency-guard task-2, format-grammar task-5) did NOT leak -- each exited through the normal SessionEnd path, leaving `SessionEnd hook ... failed: Hook cancelled` in stderr, no live process, and nothing referencing their state directories. So the leak is PATH-DEPENDENT, not universal: a worker that reaches SessionEnd exits, and one abandoned mid-flight or hard-killed does not. That is precisely why the accumulation is invisible -- the common, healthy path looks clean, so an operator who spot-checks after a normal run sees nothing wrong and concludes the fleet tidies up after itself. Same box still carried multi-day leftovers at the time of writing, including a `.claude/jobs/<id>/tmp/fleet_watch.sh` running three days and several `claude` / `claude --resume` processes aged one to two days, none of which any planwright command lists or reaps. Two fixes, and the first is not optional dressing for the second: (1) give fleet-streamjson.sh a first-class `stop <worker>` / `reap [--all]` that terminates the supervisor AND its children (SIGTERM then SIGKILL after a grace period, matching on the state-dir path rather than a bare process name so it cannot over-match an operator session), and have the tower call it when a unit completes; (2) until that lands, a conductor must sweep periodically rather than at a threshold -- waiting until the count is alarming is what produced the 95 hours. Worth a planwright issue. Related: the fleet oracle has no notion of a worker that is alive-but-finished, which is the same blind spot that let two completed workers sit unread for two hours the same session (a monitor watching only for blocked and dead workers misses yielded-idle).
