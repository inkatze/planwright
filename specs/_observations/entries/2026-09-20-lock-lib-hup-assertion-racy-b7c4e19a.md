- 2026-09-20 [planwright] tests/test-lock-lib.sh section 25's HUP arm is racy by construction and fails the whole gate under load: `FAIL: a HUP'd holder exits 129 (expected '129', got '0')`. Reproduced twice on the same branch, in consecutive full-gate runs at 785s and 831s (`lint:shell` alone reporting 780s then 815s, against a ~716s whole-gate norm), on a branch whose only diff is three prose lines in docs/fleet.md, so nothing in the change can reach lock-lib. The holder loops 200 times over `sleep 0.1`, about 20 seconds, then exits 0; the parent waits for the holder's ready file and sends SIGHUP immediately after. Exit 0 is only reachable if the holder completed all 200 sleeps before the parent got scheduled to deliver the signal, which under gate-level contention it does. An instrumented replica confirms the mechanism: 5 of 5 trials in isolation report `alive_before_kill=alive` and `wait_status=129`, and the failing shape needs the parent starved for the holder's full 20-second lifetime. Ruled out along the way: fork exhaustion (no `cannot fork` or `resource temporarily unavailable` anywhere in either gate log) and a broken `sleep` (a PATH=/nonexistent variant exits 9 from a failed `pw_lock_acquire`, not 0). Six concurrent copies under 8 CPU hogs pass 6 of 6, so CPU contention alone does not reproduce it; the gate's 212-file fork-and-IO contention does. The assertion also never checks the holder is still alive before demanding 129, so a lost race is indistinguishable from a contract violation. Worth having the holder block indefinitely rather than for a bounded 20s, and asserting liveness before the kill so the test reports a lost race as a skip rather than a failure.
