Both share a shape worth naming: an absolute millisecond bound asserted against a machine the suite itself saturates, with 212 test files running concurrently. CI is the reference runner and is clean, so the bounds hold there; locally they turn every parallel-worker session into a red gate that has to be triaged and explained per PR, which is exactly the cost that trains a reader to skim past a real failure. Worth either scaling such bounds by an observed baseline (measure an uncontended operation at suite start, express the bound as a multiple) or marking the timing-sensitive cases so the local runner reports them separately from correctness failures, rather than raising the numbers until they stop firing.
