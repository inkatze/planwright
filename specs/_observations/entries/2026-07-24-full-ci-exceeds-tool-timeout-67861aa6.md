- 2026-07-24 [planwright] mise run check now takes ~630-805s wall clock (observed twice during merge-currency-guard Task 1: 629s and 805s), which exceeds the 600s ceiling on a single foreground Bash tool call. A worker that runs the mandatory full-CI step the obvious way gets its call cut off and moved to background, and the natural next move (yield and wait for the completion notification) is exactly the stream-json stall this fleet keeps hitting, where status reports "completed success" while the worker sits idle with work stranded. Workaround used here: nohup the run to a log file and poll synchronously with sleep loops, re-entering the poll after the poll itself times out. Worth making the ergonomic path safe rather than relying on each worker to rediscover it: e.g. a documented long-CI polling recipe in the execute-task CI step, a mise task that runs the suite detached and exposes a cheap status probe, or splitting check into a fast pre-PR tier plus a slower full tier. The two slowest single files (test-check-instructions.sh 629s, test-release-checklist.sh 450s) dominate and may themselves be worth a look.
