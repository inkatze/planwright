- 2026-09-21 [planwright] A tower-loop fixture step costs about 1700 process spawns, and roughly 45 percent of them are a second and third resolution of the same config knobs: settle resolves about 12, next about 14, catchup about 6, each through config-get into resolve-config-knob into two resolve-overlay-root passes, milliseconds apart on the same file. That is why a scripted session of two dozen steps runs 180 seconds under a concurrent gate and had to be split across two files to stay inside the per-file budget, and it is most of why the shell suite now sits at 592 of its 600-second total. The work is already shared once (the settling pass serves the delivery); what is not shared is the process startup. A queue verb that ran settle, catchup and next in one process, or a per-process knob cache, would take roughly 40 percent of the forks out of every tower-loop test and every live loop iteration without changing any behaviour the wiring owns.
